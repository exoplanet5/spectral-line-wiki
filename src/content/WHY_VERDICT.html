Verdict. Quote rest wavelengths to 10⁻³ Å (59.96 m/s at 5000 Å) wherever the source supports it. At that precision a catalogue stops limiting single-line measurements at R ≳ 30 000, the 2–12 mÅ physical shifts of §3.3 are resolved instead of rounded away, and laboratory data exist for most lines (after the ASD upgrade 78 % of the element lines on this page have ≥ 3 decimals, and 35 % a stated or implied σ ≤ 1 mÅ). It is still 6× (rest wavelengths for α variation) to ~6000× (comb calibration) too coarse for differential, comb-calibrated work. It is also ≈ 9× (Gaia RVS systematics) to 3500× (DESI redshift errors) finer than survey redshifts need.

Where 1 mÅ is necessary

  • Line-by-line work at R ≳ 30 000. At SNR 100 per pixel one unblended line centroids to 0.14–0.62 mÅ (rule of thumb; 0.18–0.73 mÅ photon-noise bound for a 50 %-deep line) in the 13 optical echelle modes of §3.2. The rule FWHM/(SNR·√Npix) reaches 1 mÅ at R ≈ 29 000 (3 px per FWHM, 5000 Å; derived) Bouchy et al. 2001. A rest wavelength used for identification, deblending, a wavelength solution or a lab-to-sky comparison must be at least that good.
  • Physical shifts of 2–12 mÅ. At 5000 Å these are Earth rotation 7.8 mÅ, solar gravitational redshift 10.6 mÅ González Hernández et al. 2020, convective blueshift 0 to −9.1 mÅ depending on line depth Reiners et al. 2016 and the Zeeman σ shift at 1 kG for g = 1, 11.7 mÅ. At their own wavelengths the Ca II K and Mg II isotope shifts are −9.0 and −8.1 mÅ Murphy & Berengut 2014, and the telluric O₂ pressure shift is +2.1 mÅ. On a 10 mÅ grid each of them is about one rounding step; on a 1 mÅ grid each spans 2–12 steps.
  • Catalogue rounding. Rounding to 0.01 Å alone costs 2.9 mÅ rms (173 m/s). When ASD has a finer value, the Handbook's 2-decimal wavelengths differ from it by a median 4.0 mÅ (305 m/s; 95th percentile 22 mÅ, 1.9 km/s; 2 572 lines). The 3-decimal values differ by a median 0.5 mÅ (2 070 lines) Sansonetti & Martin 2005; Kramida et al. 2024.

Where 1 mÅ is not enough

  • EPRV. 10 cm/s is 1.7 × 10⁻⁶ Å at 5000 Å, and the Earth-twin reflex of the Sun is 9 cm/s Fischer et al. 2016.
  • Laser frequency combs. Two independent combs on HARPS agree to ≈ 1 cm/s, which is 1.7 × 10⁻⁷ Å Probst et al. 2020.
  • α variation. Laboratory rest wavelengths are needed to ≲ 20 m/s, i.e. 1.6 × 10⁻⁴ Å at Fe II 2383 Å Murphy & Berengut 2014. A change Δα/α = 10⁻⁶ moves the 43 many-multiplet lines by −0.20 to +0.12 mÅ (−30 to +19 m/s; derived).
  • Redshift drift. The Planck 2018 cosmology gives −5.4 cm/s per decade at z = 4, i.e. −1.1 × 10⁻⁶ Å per decade at the observed Lyα wavelength of 6078 Å (derived) Planck Collaboration 2020; Liske et al. 2008 give ≈ 6 cm/s per decade.

These measurements are differential. A comb or a comb-referenced etalon defines the scale, and a catalogue wavelength only fixes the zero point. The page therefore stores every source string in full (Murphy & Berengut tabulate 6–9 decimals); the 3-decimal display does not truncate the stored value.

Where 1 mÅ is overkill

  • Redshift surveys at R ≈ 1800–6500 (DESI, BOSS, LAMOST-LRS, PFS, WEAVE-LR, 4MOST-LRS) centroid single lines to 6–20 mÅ at SNR 100 (§3.2).
  • DESI requirements. σz < 0.0005(1+z) random (150 km/s) and 0.0002(1+z) systematic (60 km/s) correspond to 3.5 Å and 1.4 Å at 7000 Å DESI Collaboration 2022.
  • Stellar surveys. Their velocity requirements correspond to 9–18 mÅ, so 0.01 Å rest wavelengths are enough. 4MOST needs < 1 km/s (18 mÅ at 5450 Å) de Jong et al. 2019. LAMOST-MRS reaches ≈ 1 km/s (17 mÅ) Liu et al. 2020. Gaia RVS is required to reach ≤ 300 m/s systematic error after calibration by the end of the mission (8.6 mÅ at 8600 Å) Cropper et al. 2018.
  • Still mandatory for surveys: the medium (vacuum − air = 0.65–6.0 Å), the velocity convention (radio and optical differ by 0.3 km/s already at z = 0.001) and multiplicative (1+z) composition (§7.8).

Display and storage rule

  • Store the source string verbatim, with its medium, conversion formula and stated σ. Display 3 decimals only when the source has them and never pad: 43 % of the Handbook lines have 2 decimals. Keep 4–9 decimals in the detail view where the source gives them (Ritz values, Th and U atlases, Murphy & Berengut 2014).
  • Read a precision as a velocity. A 1 mÅ step is 250 m/s at 1200 Å, 60 m/s at 5000 Å and 14 m/s at 2.2 µm. For a uniform 50 m/s target the far-UV needs 4 decimals, while 3 decimals are generous in the K band.
  • Name the air formula. The standard formulas differ by ≤ 0.21 mÅ from 3000 Å to 1 µm and by ≈ 0.6 mÅ at 2000 Å and 2.2 µm. These differences are below the display digit but not negligible. Laboratory air differs from standard air by 26 mÅ (§3.3).

The correction chain on this page is never the limiting term at 1 mÅ. The barycentric calculator (§7.4) implements eq. 28 of Wright & Eastman 2014 and matches astropy to 0.26 mm/s rms, about 4 × 10⁻⁶ mÅ at 5000 Å. It keeps the sub-mÅ terms: the Sun's motion about the barycentre (0.27 mÅ), the lunar reflex (0.21 mÅ) and the observer-clock term (0.08 mÅ). Only EPRV needs them, but including them costs nothing.
Caveats. The centroid figures are photon-noise limits for one isolated, unresolved line. They exclude read noise, blends, LSF errors, pixel-level systematics (HARPS floor ≈ 0.15 mÅ Lovis & Pepe 2007) and wavelength-solution errors, so real single-line accuracies are worse. The Handbook–ASD statistics are a snapshot of the 2026-11-23 build. HITRAN uncertainty codes are ranges, and their upper bounds (used here) are conservative. Telluric pressure shifts use one S·u-weighted pressure per line and ignore line mixing and the temperature dependence of δair.
