Two complementary tables follow. (a) is the modelled line list, with laboratory or theoretical positions and climatological intensities. (b) is the deepest observed high-resolution night-sky atlas. Use (a) for wavelengths and (b) for what a real echelle spectrum looks like: line detectability, blends, widths and fluxes.

(a) PALACE v1.0 model line list: positions to 0.001 Å

PALACE models the Paranal airglow from ≈ 10⁵ X-shooter spectra (October 2009 – September 2019), UVES data and theoretical molecular and atomic data Noll et al. 2025. It comprises 26 541 lines over 0.3–2.5 µm and three unresolved continua.

  • Positions are vacuum values printed to 10⁻⁷ µm (0.001 Å). For OH, O₂ b–X, a–X and the isotopologues they come from HITRAN2020. For the O₂ Herzberg I (A–X), Chamberlain (A′–a), Herzberg II (c–X) and c–b systems they come from the Cosby et al. 2006 models. For atoms they come from NIST ASD. The λair column of table (a) is derived with Peck & Reeder 1972, the page's default conversion. The “model λair” column and the obs − model offsets of table (b) use Edlén 1966, the formula PALACE uses. The two differ by ≤ 0.04 mÅ over 3000–10 500 Å (derived; §7.2).
  • Intensities are reference values I in rayleigh: nocturnal annual mean, solar flux 100 sfu, zenith, before lower-atmosphere extinction. T is the zenith transmission for 2.5 mm precipitable water vapour, and I·T is roughly what reaches the ground. The O₂ A and B bands are self-absorbed: of the 6.5 kR emitted in b–X (0–0), only 1.6 R reaches the ground (derived).
  • Coverage: 3000–10 500 Å holds 15 745 lines: 12 688 OH, 1303 O₂ b–X, 934 A–X, 565 A′–a, 76 c–b, 15 c–X, 130 O I, 28 H I, and 2 each of N I, Na I and K I. Of these, 1015 have I·T ≥ 1 R and 2587 have I·T ≥ 0.1 R. Molecular lines that are also in the near-IR list of §5.4 (O₂ longward of 7400 Å, OH longward of 9000 Å) are listed there with HITRAN2024 positions. The table below holds the remaining 12 966 lines (OH, O₂ and isotopologue lines outside those ranges, and all atomic lines) plus the 183 PALACE atomic lines above 10 500 Å, 13 149 rows in all.
  • Atomic lines beyond 10 500 Å: all 183 are O I radiative-recombination lines (layer 300 km) at 10 678–21 337 Å. They sum to 35.5 R, or 18.6 R at the ground (derived). The strongest multiplets are 3d ³D–4f ³F at 18 248.3–18 248.8 Å (14.9 R), 3p ³P–3d ³D at 11 289.4–11 290.4 Å (5.5 R), 3d ⁵D–4f ⁵F at 18 025.8–18 026.9 Å (5.4 R), and 3d ³D–5f ³F at 12 573 Å and 3p ⁵P–4s ⁵S at 11 298–11 305 Å (2.7 R each). PALACE measured only the 777 and 845 nm features. It scales all other recombination lines with theoretical recombination coefficients (Escalante & Victor 1992, optically thin, 1000 K) and NIST A values, so these intensities are model values. PALACE has no lines of other atoms above 10 500 Å.
  • PALACE supersedes the ESO Sky Model airglow list of 4764 lines Noll et al. 2012.
0.001 Å is the rounding step, not the accuracy, which follows the parent database. HITRAN2020 assigns position code 2 (0.01–0.1 cm⁻¹) to 21 207 of its 21 714 ¹⁶OH lines in 3000–9000 Å (derived). That is formally 2.5–25 mÅ at 5000 Å and 8–81 mÅ at 9000 Å. Empirically the agreement is much better: the Keck/HIRES laboratory OH wavelengths (Abrams et al. 1994 FTS) minus PALACE give a median of +0.32 mÅ and σ = 1.8 mÅ for the 804 lines within 5 mÅ (derived). The blue O₂ model positions were fitted to the same UVES data as atlas (b), so they are not independent of it.

(b) UVES/VLT observed atlas: positions good to ≈ 17 mÅ

Hanuschik 2003 stacked UVES sky exposures of up to 16 h on the 8.2 m UT2. The atlas covers 3140–10 430 Å at R ≈ 45 000 (blue) and 43 000 (red) with a 1″ slit. The CDS tables 4–9 hold 2809 lines. The later ESO QC web tables add 59: 21 at 3764–3799 Å, 3 at 6714–6718 Å, and 35 at 8542–8637 Å from the 800U gap-filler setting. Lines measured in two overlapping settings (41 groups) and three lines printed twice in the source table appear once, leaving 2824 primary lines. No line table exists for 5770–5830 Å, and 8560–8600 Å is only partly covered.

  • Wavelengths are air values on the UVES ThAr pipeline scale, printed to 0.1 mÅ. The 28 primary lines found only in the ESO web tables keep their 6-decimal strings; beyond ≈ 0.2–1 mÅ those digits are float32 rounding noise. The internal dispersion-solution rms is 4.7 mÅ (346 nm setting) to 10 mÅ (860 nm).
  • UVES − Keck = +8 ± 17 mÅ for 529 lines longward of 5200 Å. Hanuschik gives 17 mÅ as the likely error of a single line position, 0.73 km s⁻¹ at 7000 Å. Averaging the 58 lines in 7000–7100 Å would calibrate a spectrum to ≈ 2 mÅ rms. That figure is statistical only: in this 860L range the atlas positions lie +14.7 mÅ above the model positions (median of 46 S1/L2 lines, derived), so calibrate on the model λair column.
  • “Peak flux” is the Gaussian-fit peak flux density including the local continuum, in 10⁻¹⁶ erg s⁻¹ cm⁻² Å⁻¹ arcsec⁻². At 3550.38 Å, one unit is 95.56 R nm⁻¹ (derived). Two lines, 3311.4404 and 7933.2573 Å, have a negative published FWHM (−0.0613 and −0.3152 Å). This is a fit artefact; both rows are kept as published.

Historical: the Keck/HIRES atlases

Osterbrock et al. 1996 and 1997 identified 969 OH lines in Mauna Kea HIRES sky spectra. They are given as laboratory or calculated air wavelengths, to 0.001 Å, from Abrams et al. 1994 FTS data (accurate to ≈ 10⁻⁵ Å; Edlén 1953 air), together with 45 computed O₂ b–X(0–1) lines (≈ 0.01 Å). The atlas was conceived for echelle wavelength calibration. For e/f doublets closer than 0.2 Å the blend error is “well below 0.01 Å”. Against HITRAN2020 (via PALACE) the HIRES values agree to +0.32 ± 1.8 mÅ for the 804 lines within 5 mÅ. 37 rows differ by more than 0.02 Å; these are probably catalogue typos or e/f label conventions, kept verbatim and flagged. The matching HIRES wavelength appears in the UVES table (“Keck/HIRES λ”).
