The table below lists 8 455 solar lines from 3373.65 to 12818.33 Å, all in standard air. All are absorption lines except two coronal emission lines, which are flagged. They fall at 7 920 distinct wavelengths: APGL98 and APGL98b give 483 solar features two to five times (535 extra rows), once for each candidate identification. There are 102 identification labels, from Fe I (3 331 rows), Cr I, Ti I and Ni I to the molecular CN, C₂ and CH lines. The rows are built on the modern FTS line-centre catalogue. Photographic disc-centre lines fill the gaps and extend the range to 1.28 µm. Where the laboratory wavelength is known, it is given in the next columns together with the solar − lab shift.

Coverage. The list stops at 3374 Å and 12 818 Å (Pa β, the last Babcock & Moore line in the digitisation), while the telluric list (§6.3) reaches 25 000 Å. There are no solar lines here in the H and K bands, nor in the J band beyond 12 818 Å: for example, the APOGEE H-band lines and Fe I 15648.5 Å (g = 3) are absent. Below 3374 Å only the lettered lines of §6.1 are given.

How the columns were filled

  • σλ. For APGL98 rows this is the catalogue's eλ, the “std. deviation from the 4th-order polynomial fit” (VizieR). Its median is 3.8 mÅ and the interquartile range 2.6–5.4 mÅ (derived), larger than the precision quoted in the abstract. MMH and Babcock & Moore publish no σ. For these rows the table uses 2.5 units of the last printed digit, the lower bound NIST ASD gives for implied uncertainties, and at least 4 mÅ, the scatter of MMH against APGL98 (below). The same rule applies to the 71 rows taken from APGL98b alone, which also have no σ.
  • Kitt Peak wavelength scale. The flux atlas was anchored on the telluric O₂ line at 688.38 nm; most other scans were aligned to it through solar lines in their overlaps. Against the IAG atlas it agrees within ±10 m/s over 500–700 nm, with a 10 m/s step at 576 nm. The offset grows to about −50 m/s at 430 nm and −20 m/s at 850 nm, and Kurucz et al. estimated errors of up to 100 m/s in their bluest scan (Reiners et al. 2016). For the 1 250 rows with both, APGL98 flux λ minus the IAG-based solar λ (lab × (1 + (vcb + 636 m/s)/c)) is +0.3 mÅ in the median (derived).
  • Laboratory λ. A laboratory wavelength is attached only if its lower-level excitation potential agrees with the catalogue EP within 0.02 eV. Without this test, one solar feature listed for two transitions of the same species would give both the same lab λ.
    • Fe I lines use Nave et al. 1994, through APGL98b: 1 448 rows, 1 445 distinct lines. For 132 APGL98 Fe I rows, APGL98b assigns the feature to an Fe I line with a different EP, so its lab λ is not attached to them.
    • The other 1 954 rows are matched to NIST ASD v5.12 lines of the same species (Kramida et al. 2024) within −15 to +25 mÅ of the solar λ, whose lower-level energy Ei (cm⁻¹ ÷ 8065.544 = eV) is within 0.02 eV of the EP. When several lines pass, the one nearest +0.3 km/s is taken (15 rows).
    • 1 606 APGL98 rows have no lab λ. For 1 273 of them NIST has no line of the species in the window, for 176 only lines with a different EP, and for 157 only lines without level energies.
    • At 11 features APGL98 lists two entries of one species whose EPs both pass the test. Both get the same lab λ, and the CSV notes this.
    • The EP test does not prove an identification in a blend.
    Sun − lab is the flux λ minus the lab λ, as a velocity. Its median is +236 m/s for Fe I (Nave) and +212 m/s for the NIST-matched lines (derived).
  • IAG. The lambda column of IAG Table A.1 (Nave et al. rest wavelengths) is in vacuum nm, although VizieR does not say so. All 1 251 values match the air laboratory wavelengths to < 1 mÅ only after vacuum-to-air conversion. The convective-blueshift column follows Reiners et al. and excludes the gravitational redshift. Depths are flux depths, as a fraction of the continuum.
  • MMH and Babcock & Moore wavelengths. The values come from the HS5000 digitisation, a subset of 4 965 MMH lines (the deepest line per 0.5 Å) and 1 016 B&M lines. Each MMH value was checked against the OCR text of NBS Monograph 61:
    • 3 953 match exactly;
    • 249 match once trailing zeros dropped by the digitisation are restored, and 32 once a zero it added is removed. The table gives the printed string, and its decimals set σλ;
    • 704 fall where the OCR layer has gaps;
    • 17 differ slightly from the nearest OCR token (“OCR ?” in the table);
    • 10 were read on the page images. Six are integers in the digitisation whose printed decimals were lost (on these pages the decimal point is faint or missing, e.g. “5698 340m”); the printed values are restored. Four are confirmed errors, kept verbatim but flagged:
      • Mg b1 is 5183.619, not 5183.600;
      • Hβ is 4861.342 with EW 3680, not 4861.35 / 3000;
      • Fe I 5168.91 has EW 114, not 700;
      • the He D3 entry (5875.62) is not in MMH.
    Of the B&M values, 23 have four decimals, more than B&M printed (where MMH reprints them, it gives 3 decimals), so their precision is taken as 3 decimals. Ten are integers with unknown decimals. Both groups are flagged. MMH reprints B&M wavelengths above 6600 Å: 301 of the 425 B&M rows below 8770 Å appear verbatim in the MMH OCR text.
  • Identifications. The Balmer and Paschen lines, given in LaTeX notation in the digitisation, are written as H I (Hα), H I (H8), H I (Pa β) and so on. The HS5000 code “T I” (6 MMH lines) was read on the page images: three are Ti I (5585.663, 5739.061, 6996.634), one is Fe I (4821.601), and two have no identification (5389.847, 6017.00). MMH also has no identification for 7311.64 (HS5000 “O”), and gives 6397.545 as telluric (“Atm”). Fe X 6374.56 and Ni XV 6701.47 are coronal emission lines that HS5000 added; they are not in MMH and are flagged in the table. The B&M codes “c” (10235.03, 10312.55) and “Cp” (11602.08, 11630.5) are not species. No B&M page images are available here, so they are listed as unidentified.
  • Precision of MMH. For 2 515 rows in both catalogues, MMH runs +2.1 mÅ above the APGL98 disc-centre λ in the median, with 68 % between −1.9 and +6.3 mÅ (derived). MMH values are therefore good to a few mÅ.
Solar wavelengths are not laboratory wavelengths. Every λ in this table is measured in the solar spectrum. It includes the gravitational redshift (+633 m/s seen from 1 au) and a convective shift that depends on the line (§6.4). For Fe I the flux wavelengths lie +236 m/s above the laboratory values in the median, with a standard deviation of 284 m/s (1 445 lines, derived). That is +3.9 ± 4.7 mÅ at 5000 Å. Flux (APGL98, IAG) and disc-centre (MMH, B&M and the second APGL98 value) wavelengths are different quantities. Do not use them as rest wavelengths: take those from §4 or the “Lab λ” column.
