Every process between emission and detection maps a frequency onto a frequency: expansion, peculiar motion, gravitational potentials, the barycentre's motion through the CMB, and the observer's motion about the barycentre. The (1 + z) factors therefore multiply (Davis et al. 2011, eq. 1; Wright & Eastman 2014, eqs. 10–11):

(7.8.1)1+zobs=(1+z¯)(1+zpec)(1+zgrav)(1+X)(1+zB)(1+z⊙→CMB)

z̄: cosmological redshift; zpec: peculiar motion of the source; zgrav: gravitational redshift of the source relative to the observer; X: line-formation shifts (convection, pressure, asymmetries; the (1 + X) of Lindegren & Dravins 2003); zB: barycentric correction (§7.4); z⊙→CMB: correction for the barycentre's motion relative to the CMB. Eq. 7.1 is the same chain with the refractive index of air added.

Why additive corrections fail

Adding redshifts or velocities drops the cross terms. For a correction v applied to a redshift z the error is ≈ z v/c in z, however precisely z itself is measured. The values below are derived; the last column is the Planck18 distance-modulus change caused by a redshift error of 10⁻⁴.

Δz = 10⁻⁵ is 3 km/s, i.e. 0.05 Å at 5000 Å, fifty times the 1 mÅ grid of this page (derived). Redshift systematics of cosmological interest come from formulae and frames, not from rest wavelengths. Store z rather than cz, multiply (1 + z) factors, and record the frame and apex.

CMB-frame redshift

The Planck 2018 solar dipole is 369.82 ± 0.11 km/s toward (l, b) = (264.021°, 48.253°), i.e. β = (1.23357 ± 0.00036) × 10⁻³ (Planck Collaboration 2020a). For a comoving source the boost is exact in either form:

(7.8.2)1+zCMB=(1+zobs)(1+zB)(1+z⊙→CMB),1+z⊙→CMB=γ(1+βcosθ)=1γ(1−βcosθ′)
  • θ is the angle between the source and the apex measured in the CMB frame. θ′ is the same angle computed from catalogue (barycentric) coordinates. The two differ by aberration of up to β rad = 4.24′ (derived).
  • γ − 1 = 7.61 × 10⁻⁷ (0.228 km/s) is the transverse Doppler term of the barycentre. For a source 90° from the apex it is the whole correction (derived).
  • Mixing the two forms by inserting θ′ into γ(1 + β cos θ) errs by β² sin²θ′ ≤ 1.52 × 10⁻⁶ (≤ 0.46 km/s). The line-of-sight form z⊙ = [(1 − β cos θ′)/(1 + β cos θ′)]½ − 1 used by Carr et al. 2022 errs by half that, ≤ 0.23 km/s. Both values are derived by an explicit Lorentz transformation. Both errors are an order of magnitude below the 10⁻⁵ level that matters for supernova cosmology, but they are several mÅ at 5000 Å.
  • NED's "3K" frame still uses the COBE dipole, 371.0 km/s toward (264.14°, 48.26°) (Fixsen et al. 1996). Its amplitude is 1.2 km/s larger than the Planck value (derived).
  • Is the dipole purely kinematic? Every CMB-frame correction assumes so. The number-count dipole of 1.36 million CatWISE quasars points the same way but has more than twice the expected kinematic amplitude, rejecting the purely kinematic interpretation at 4.9σ (Secrest et al. 2021). At that level the “CMB rest frame” is a convention, not a measured matter rest frame.

The redshift-composition calculator below evaluates eq. 7.8.2. It treats each entry as an exact factor 1 + v/c, so enter vB = c zB from §7.4, which already contains γ and the observer's gravitational term, and the CMB term as c z⊙→CMB. If you enter only the projection v⊙ cos θ (the "Correction Δv" column of §7.7), the second-order terms β²(cos²θ′ − ½), up to ±7.6 × 10⁻⁷ (±0.23 km/s), are left out. With the default inputs (zobs = 0.05, vB = 25 km/s, CMB term 300 km/s) the naive sum zobs + vB/c + vCMB/c is low by 5.4 × 10⁻⁵, i.e. 16 km/s in cz (derived).

Peculiar velocities and the H₀ error budget

A peculiar redshift is a ratio, 1 + zpec = (1 + z)/(1 + z̄), so to first order vpec = c (z − z̄)/(1 + z̄). The common form vpec = cz − H₀D overestimates peculiar velocities by ≈ 100 km/s at z ≈ 0.04 (Davis & Scrimgeour 2014). Velocity noise σv propagates into the distance modulus as follows (Davis et al. 2011, eqs. A1, A4; empty-universe form):

With a local escape speed of 528 (+24/−25) km/s (Deason et al. 2019), |Φ|/c ≳ vesc²/(2c) ≈ 0.47 km/s (7.8 mÅ at 5000 Å, derived): light from outside the Galaxy arrives blueshifted by at least that much. It is nearly constant over the sky and years, so it is absorbed into extragalactic redshifts and cancels for Galactic sources at similar potential, but it is not part of zB.
  • Solar lines formed higher in the atmosphere are less redshifted: about 26 m/s less for a line formed 30 Mm up (GM⊙/c · [1/R⊙ − 1/(R⊙ + 30 Mm)], derived; Lindegren & Dravins quote “some 20 m/s”), only ≈ 2 m/s for the top of the chromosphere (2 Mm), perhaps 100 m/s less for coronal lines. For single stars vg can rarely be computed to better than 50 m/s (Lindegren & Dravins 2003).
  • Dwarfs versus giants. Solar-type dwarfs should be redshifted about 500 m/s more than giants. In M67, however, dwarfs and giants share the same apparent RV to ≈ 100 m/s, because the stronger convective blueshift of dwarfs compensates (Pasquini et al. 2011; §6.4).
  • Clusters. About 10 km/s is expected for ~10¹⁴ M⊙ clusters (Wojtak et al. 2011). The measured 7.7 km/s corresponds to Δz = 2.6 × 10⁻⁵ (derived), above the 10⁻⁵ level that biases supernova cosmology (Calcino & Davis 2017).

Redshift drift (Sandage–Loeb test)

The redshift of a comoving source changes with the observer's time t₀ (Sandage 1962; Loeb 1998; Liske et al. 2008, eq. 4):

(7.8.6)z˙≡dzdt0=(1+z)H0−H(z)
(7.8.7)v˙=cz˙1+z=cH0[1−E(z)1+z],E(z)=H(z)H0

For Planck18 (Planck Collaboration 2020b; H₀ = 67.66 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹, Ωm = 0.30966, with photons and massive neutrinos, as in astropy), cH₀ = 2.074 cm/s per year. The drift is positive at low z, crosses zero at z = 1.960 and peaks at +2.58 cm/s per decade near z ≈ 0.64. A region with ż > 0 is "the hallmark of ΩΛ ≠ 0" (Liske et al. 2008).

Lyα-forest peculiar accelerations are more than an order of magnitude below the cosmological signal (Cooke 2020).
  • The barycentre itself accelerates. Gaia EDR3 measures the Solar-System acceleration as (2.32 ± 0.16) × 10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻² toward (α, δ) = (269.1°, −31.6°), close to the Galactic centre (Gaia Collaboration, Klioner et al. 2021). Projected on the line of sight this gives every distant source an apparent drift −a·n̂ of up to ±7.3 mm/s per year (±7.3 cm/s per decade, derived) — larger than the cosmological signal at z ≈ 2–3. Drift experiments must model it as a dipole; the barycentric frame is not inertial at this level.
  • ELT/ANDES. The experiment needs Doppler drifts measured to about 1 cm/s per year (Steinmetz et al. 2008). For ANDES the residual bias of the wavelength solution "may not drift by more than ∼1 cm/s" over about two decades, against design goals of σ < 0.5 m/s, accuracy < 1 m/s and stability < 1 cm/s. The redshift-drift case is a goal, not a requirement (Martins et al. 2024). The signal is 10⁻⁷–10⁻⁶ Å over 20 years, 10⁻⁴–10⁻³ of this page's 1 mÅ grid. Only a laser frequency comb can deliver it (§7.9).
The in-page drift calculator uses E(z) = [Ωm(1+z)³ + Ωk(1+z)² + ΩΛ]½ with Ωk = 1 − Ωm − ΩΛ, and a Julian year. It has no radiation and no neutrino term. With its defaults (H₀ = 67.66, Ωm = 0.3097, ΩΛ = 0.6903, flat) it returns +2.28, −0.06, −2.74 and −5.30 cm/s per decade at z = 1, 2, 3 and 4. These are 0.03–0.07 cm/s per decade (1–2 %) above astropy's Planck18 values. The relative difference is large only near the zero crossing, which moves from z = 1.960 to 1.978. Over 20 years the difference is 0.1–0.15 cm/s at z = 3–4, an order of magnitude below the forecast of eq. 7.8.8 (all derived).

  • Sensitivity. Across all 43 transitions of Murphy & Berengut (2014), 1 ppm shifts lines by −30.0 to +19.4 m/s. The Zn II 2026 – Cr II 2066 pair separates by 47.6 m/s per ppm. A 1 mÅ error in the Zn II 2026 rest wavelength (148 m/s) mimics Δα/α ≈ 5 ppm (all derived).
  • Laboratory requirement. A transition is "useful for measuring Δα/α" only if its rest frequency is known to "δv ≲ 20 m s⁻¹" (Murphy & Berengut 2014). That is 1.1 × 10⁻⁴ Å at 1608 Å, 1.35 × 10⁻⁴ Å at 2026 Å and 1.9 × 10⁻⁴ Å at 2796 Å (derived), 5–10 × finer than the 1 mÅ grid. Tabulated uncertainties run from 0.04 m/s for the comb-calibrated Mg II isotopes to ≈ 20 m/s for Ni II. Isotopic and hyperfine structure must be modelled: ²⁴Mg → ²⁶Mg moves Mg II 2796 by −8.05 mÅ (−863 m/s; §3.3).
  • Only relative shifts matter. Errors in q cannot create a spurious non-zero Δα/α. Velocity shifts between transitions can, whether they come from laboratory wavelengths or from calibration distortions. UVES spectra calibrated with the older Th-Ar list contain ±30–75 m/s distortions (Murphy et al. 2007a). ESPRESSO's Th-Ar+FP and LFC solutions differ by up to 24 m/s (Schmidt et al. 2021).
  • Results. Δα/α = (−1.1 ± 0.4) × 10⁻⁵ from 30 Keck absorbers (Webb et al. 1999). (−0.543 ± 0.116) × 10⁻⁵ from 128 absorbers (Murphy, Webb & Flambaum 2003). A claimed spatial dipole toward RA 17.3 h, Dec −61° of amplitude 0.97 (+0.22/−0.20) × 10⁻⁵ at 4.1σ (King et al. 2012; 4.2σ in Webb et al. 2011). The LFC-calibrated ESPRESSO result for HE 0515−4414 (z = 1.15) is 1.3 ± 1.3 (stat) ± 0.4 (sys) ppm (Murphy et al. 2022).
  • ANDES aims at ≈ 0.3 ppm, i.e. ∼6 m/s relative line shifts. That needs relative line positions accurate to ∼1 m/s, whereas "ESPRESSO's accuracy in this context is ∼20 m s⁻¹" (Martins et al. 2024).

  • H₂ Lyman and Werner lines have sensitivity coefficients Ki of order −0.02 to +0.05, e.g. −0.01872 for L0 P(4) and +0.04417 for L11 R(0) (Ubachs et al. 2007). For Δμ/μ = 1 ppm these two lines shift by −5.6 and +13.2 m/s (derived).

Summary. Compose redshifts as products of (1 + z) and use exact boosts with stated apices: additive shortcuts err by z v/c, which is 10⁻³ at z = 1. Gravitational redshifts run from 0.6 km/s (Sun) to 30–80 km/s (white dwarfs) and about 10 km/s (clusters), and they stay inside any measured redshift. Redshift drift (cm/s per decade) and α/μ tests (m/s per ppm) are differential measurements. They need rest wavelengths to ~10⁻⁴ Å and a frequency-comb wavelength scale (§7.9), not just the 1 mÅ catalogue values of this page.
