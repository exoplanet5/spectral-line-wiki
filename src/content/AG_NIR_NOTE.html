This is one merged, de-duplicated list of 15 802 lines, one row per Λ-doublet component or O₂ line. HITRAN lists the electric-quadrupole and magnetic-dipole O₂ lines separately (387 rows are E2), so some O₂ rows share a wavelength. Up to 2.5 µm it holds 13 466 lines: 11 545 ¹⁶OH and 12 ¹⁸OH lines at 9000–25 000 Å, and 1434 ¹⁶O₂, 462 ¹⁶O¹⁸O and 13 ¹⁶O¹⁷O lines at 7400–25 000 Å. The O₂ bands are a–X (0–0) at 1.27 µm, (0–1) at 1.58 µm, (1–0) and (1–1); b–X (0–0) at 762 nm, (0–1) at 865 nm and weak hot bands; and the Noxon b–a (0–0) band at 1.91 µm. Of these, 3851 OH lines (2233 ≥ 10 R, 769 ≥ 100 R) and 557 O₂ lines have reference intensities ≥ 1 R. Beyond 2.5 µm the list adds 2336 ¹⁶OH lines at 2.50–4.20 µm, without intensities: the fundamental (Δv = 1) bands (1–0) to (9–8), and 69 high-N lines of the Δv = 2 bands (7–5) to (9–7) (see the OH fundamental section below).

Positions: HITRAN2024

  • Positions come from HITRANonline line-by-line data, HITRAN2024 edition Gordon et al. 2026. Wavenumbers are kept verbatim, and λvac = 10⁸/ν̃ is derived and printed to 4 decimals.
  • ¹⁶OH Meinel positions are unchanged since HITRAN2020 Gordon et al. 2022. They are a PGOPHER line list from the global X²Π fit of Brooke et al. 2016, refitted with the UVES airglow lines of Noll et al. 2020. Those lines agreed with Brooke's list except at the higher rotational levels of v = 5 and 6.
  • O₂ follows the HITRAN2024 update Adkins et al. 2025. The a–X (0–0) positions moved by up to 2.7 mÅ from the HITRAN2020 values used in PALACE (median −0.04 mÅ), and the Noxon band (74 lines) is new. For the 13 ¹⁶O¹⁷O b–X (0–0) lines, PALACE − HITRAN2024 is +0.4 to +11 mÅ (derived).
  • 242 rows (230 O₂ b–X lines, mostly hot and sequence bands, and 12 ¹⁸OH) exist only in PALACE. Their positions are the Cosby et al. 2006 model values, with no stated uncertainty.

Intensities: PALACE v1.0

  • “I (R)” is the PALACE reference intensity Noll et al. 2025: Cerro Paranal, zenith, nocturnal annual mean, 100 sfu, above lower-atmosphere absorption. 13 150 PALACE lines match HITRAN by quantum-number ID. Multiply I by the zenith transmission Tzen (2.5 mm PWV) to get the ground-level value.
  • Summed reference intensities are ≈ 7.1 × 10⁵ R for OH (0.9–2.5 µm) and ≈ 1.66 × 10⁵ R for O₂, dominated by a–X (0–0) with 1.55 × 10⁵ R (derived). The a–X (0–0) and b–X (0–0) bands are strongly self-absorbed at ground level. The Noxon band is dayglow, lies in a strong H₂O band and has no PALACE intensity.
  • The brightest OH lines are (4–2) Q1e/Q1f(1.5) at 15 833.3747 and 15 833.1582 Å (vacuum), ≈ 6.68 kR each.
  • The A column holds HITRAN2024 values. PALACE used corrected HITRAN2020 A values, so A and I are not mutually consistent at the per-cent level.

OH fundamental bands (Δv = 1), 2.5–4.2 µm

  • Bands with Δv = 1 lie outside the X-shooter range on which PALACE is built Noll et al. 2025. These rows therefore have HITRAN2024 positions, A and E″ Gordon et al. 2026 but no intensity (note “no PALACE intensity”), and setting the “I ≥” filter hides them. HITRAN2024 kept the HITRAN2020 ¹⁶OH wavenumbers and replaced the intensities.
  • Selection: ¹⁶OH, v′ ≤ 9, 2.5 < λvac ≤ 4.2 µm, N″ ≤ 20 and A ≥ 0.01 s⁻¹. This gives 2267 Δv = 1 lines (1455 main-branch, 812 satellite) and the 69 Δv = 2 lines. The cuts are set with the PALACE intensities of the Δv = 2–3 bands, which share most of the upper levels: N″ ≤ 15 keeps 97.8 % of the intensity and N″ ≤ 20 keeps 99.1 %. The A cut removes only weak satellites and high-J Q lines; the brightest PALACE line it would remove has 3.8 R, against 6.68 kR for the brightest line (derived).
  • Bands: (1–0) 297, (2–1) 313, (3–2) 313, (4–3) 295, (5–4) 275, (6–5) 263, (7–6) 240, (8–7) 169 and (9–8) 102 lines. The P branches continue beyond the 4.2 µm limit: with the same cuts another 12 lines of (3–2) and 134 of (9–8) lie at 4.2–5.0 µm. The bands from (3–2) upward are therefore truncated, (8–7) and (9–8) severely.
  • Accuracy: 660 rows have position code 3, 1590 code 2 and 86 code 1. By eq. (5.3) the upper bounds are 0.07–0.18 Å, 0.6–1.8 Å and 6–17 Å. At 3.5 µm 1 mÅ is 8.6 m s⁻¹, and the thermal FWHM of OH at 190 K is 0.084 Å (derived).
  • From the ground, much of 2.5–2.9 µm and 4.2–4.4 µm is opaque (H₂O, CO₂), and beyond ≈ 3.5 µm the thermal emission of the atmosphere and telescope increasingly dominates the background. The thermal-IR emitters (NO 5.3 µm, CO₂ 4.3 and 15 µm, O₃ 9.6 µm) are out of scope here.

Accuracy against the 1 mÅ target

HITRAN quotes wavenumber uncertainty codes (code 2 = 0.01–0.1 cm⁻¹, 3 = 0.001–0.01 cm⁻¹, and so on Rothman et al. 2005). In wavelength:

(5.3)Δλ=10−8λ2Δν~(λ in Å, ν~ in cm⁻¹)
  • O₂ meets the target. Almost all lines of the a–X (0–0), (0–1), b–X (0–0), (0–1), (1–1) and Noxon bands have codes 5–6 (≤ 1.6 × 10⁻⁴ Å at 1.27 µm; b–X (0–1) < 7.5 × 10⁻⁵ Å). a–X (1–0) and (1–1), plus 20 other lines, have code 4, whose upper bound is 1.6 mÅ at 1.27 µm.
  • OH does not, formally. Up to 2.5 µm, 9724 OH rows have code 2, 1685 code 3 and 136 code 1. At 1.58 µm codes 3–2 mean 2.5–250 mÅ. The Brooke et al. fit has an average line-position residual of 0.026 cm⁻¹ over all data, including the UV B–X lines (≈ 6.5 mÅ at 1.58 µm, derived). The vibrational term values are known to ≈ 10⁻³ cm⁻¹.
  • Realistic scale for OH: 1–6 mÅ at low J. Independent model lists agree with HITRAN at this level (table below). These lists rest on overlapping laboratory data (FTS of Abrams et al. 1994 and later), so the agreement is internal consistency, not an absolute accuracy. At high J the older lists fail.

All comparisons are derived. Lines are matched by quantum-number label (Oliva) or by Λ-doublet matching (Rousselot, whose list has no labels). Oliva et al. 2015 OH wavelengths are computed from the Bernath & Colin 2009 constants, not measured; the GIANO calibration itself was ≈ 0.07 Å rms. Rousselot et al. 2000 computed theirs from Abrams et al. levels and state an accuracy of “a few hundredths of Å”. The −0.74 Å outlier, (5–3) P1f(22.5), lies in the high-J v′ = 5–6 levels that HITRAN2020 refitted with airglow data.

Rousselot's Q-branch Λ splittings are wrong. Individual Q components can be off by up to ≈ 24 Å at high J, so for Q lines use the Λ-doublet-mean difference, not the component column. Unresolved OH doublets are blends whose centroid depends on the e/f intensity ratio. For 1 mÅ work on OH, prefer resolved, low-J P/R lines and treat the HITRAN code interval as the formal bound.

Air wavelengths

The list is native in vacuum. The λair column is derived with Peck & Reeder 1972, the page's default conversion, and beyond 1.69 µm this extrapolates the formula. The curated file also holds a Morton 2000 conversion for every row and Rousselot's own air values for his Table 3 OH and Table 2 O₂ lines (948 rows); the table does not show either. Peck & Reeder − Morton is +0.17, +0.29 and +0.40 mÅ at 1.0, 1.6 and 2.2 µm, and Edlén 1966 (the PALACE formula) − Peck & Reeder is −0.04, −0.07 and −0.10 mÅ (derived). Rousselot's OH air values minus the Morton conversion have a median of +0.36 mÅ (max 1.0 mÅ). The vacuum − air difference itself is 2.741, 4.370 and 6.003 Å at the same wavelengths. For the 2.5–4.2 µm rows every formula is extrapolated: Peck & Reeder − Morton grows to +0.64 mÅ at 3.5 µm and +0.77 mÅ at 4.2 µm, and Ciddor 1996 − Peck & Reeder is −0.43 and −0.52 mÅ (derived), so use vacuum there. In velocity, 1 mÅ is 33.3, 23.6, 18.7, 13.6 and 8.6 m s⁻¹ at 0.9, 1.27, 1.6, 2.2 and 3.5 µm. The thermal FWHM at 190 K is 0.038 Å for OH at 1.6 µm and 0.022 Å for O₂ at 1.27 µm (derived).
