The following effects move a measured line away from its laboratory wavelength, or move a catalogue value away from what the spectrum shows. Sizes are typical magnitudes at 5000 Å unless a different wavelength is stated; 1 mÅ = 59.96 m/s and 1 m/s = 1.67 × 10⁻⁵ Å there. The Level column says where an effect starts to matter. ≫ 1 mÅ: always. 1–10 mÅ: at the precision of this page. < 1 mÅ: only for m/s work. cm/s: only for EPRV and cosmology.

The time-stamp row adds the Earth's rotational (0.034 m/s²) and orbital (0.006 m/s²) accelerations, consistent with the 250 ms timing requirement for 1 cm/s of Wright & Eastman 2014.

At 1 mÅ (60 m/s at 5000 Å) you must handle explicitly: the medium and the state of the air; the barycentric correction including Earth rotation; the frame and multiplicative composition; gravitational and convective shifts; isotopes, hyperfine structure and Zeeman splitting; blends; and telluric and sky-line zero points. The "< 1 mÅ" rows (heliocentric vs barycentric, lunar reflex, relativistic and clock terms, timing, transverse Doppler, calibrator distortions) still add up to tens of m/s and decide m/s-level work.
Never mix lists: laboratory (rest, vacuum or standard-air), solar (gravitationally redshifted and convectively shifted), telluric (topocentric and pressure-shifted) and sky-line atlas wavelengths (instrument zero point) are different quantities. Each list on this page states its medium, frame and convention.
