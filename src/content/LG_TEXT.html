Outside the Milky Way, velocities are referred to the Local Group (LG) barycentre, to the cosmic microwave background (CMB), or to a model of infall onto nearby mass concentrations. The Sun moves at 300–370 km/s relative to these frames. The LG apex vectors differ by up to 87 km/s (1.45 Å at 5000 Å; 29 km/s at the worked-example direction), and even the CMB-dipole conventions differ by 1.3–2.0 km/s, or 22–33 mÅ (all derived). Both are far larger than the mÅ precision of the rest wavelengths, so every extragalactic velocity must name its apex.

Local Group barycentre

The LG barycentre is dominated by the Milky Way–M31 pair. Relative to the Milky Way, M31 has a radial velocity of −109.3 ± 4.4 km/s and a tangential velocity of 17.0 km/s (< 34.3 km/s at 1σ). The orbit is therefore close to radial, and the implied LG mass is (3.17 ± 0.57) × 10¹² M⊙ van der Marel et al. 2012. The measured apices alone carry 10–23 km/s (1σ) at that position (derived). The correction also changes across a field: Greisen et al. note it "may amount to 5 km/s … over a field of one degree" (316 km/s × 1° = 5.5 km/s, derived). Sub-km/s comparisons in an LG frame are only meaningful between data reduced with the same apex.

The CMB dipole

The CMB defines the cosmic rest frame. To first order, the Sun's velocity follows from the dipole anisotropy:

(7.7.3)β⊙=v⊙c≃ΔTdipoleT0
  • Planck 2018 (HFI): 369.82 ± 0.11 km/s toward (l, b) = (264.021° ± 0.011°, 48.253° ± 0.005°), which is (α, δ) = (167.942°, −6.944°) ± 0.007° in J2000, or 11h11m46.1s, −06°56′39″ (sexagesimal derived). β = (1.23357 ± 0.00036) × 10⁻³, and the dipole amplitude is 3362.08 ± 0.99 μK Planck Collab. 2020.
  • COBE/DMR: 3.365 ± 0.027 mK toward (264.4° ± 0.3°, 48.4° ± 0.5°) Kogut et al. 1993. With T₀ = 2.728 K this is 369.8 km/s (derived).
  • COBE/FIRAS: 3.372 ± 0.007 mK toward (264.14° ± 0.30°, 48.26° ± 0.30°) at 95 % CL, with T₀ = 2.728 ± 0.004 K Fixsen et al. 1996. That gives cΔT/T₀ = 370.56 km/s (derived). NED's "3K" frame uses 371.0 km/s toward (264.14°, +48.26°) NED.
  • FITS CMBDIPOL: the conventional magnitude is 368 km/s toward (263.85°, 48.25°) Greisen et al. 2006, after WMAP Bennett et al. 2003.
The Planck uncertainty of 0.11 km/s is 1.8 mÅ at 5000 Å. The CMB frame is therefore the only extragalactic frame defined almost to the precision of this page's wavelengths. The NED (Fixsen), COBE/DMR and FITS CMBDIPOL vectors differ from Planck by 1.3, 1.9 and 2.0 km/s respectively. At the example direction NED and Planck differ by 0.76 km/s, or 13 mÅ (all derived).

Large-scale flows

Nearby galaxies still carry peculiar velocities of hundreds of km/s in the CMB frame. Tully et al. 2008 decompose the 631 km/s motion of the Local Sheet into three components: 259 km/s away from the Local Void, 185 km/s toward the Virgo Cluster and 455 km/s on larger scales. For Hubble-flow distances, NED applies the multi-attractor infall model of Mould et al. 2000 after converting to the LG frame with the Karachentsev & Makarov (KM96) apex:

(7.7.4)Vin=Vfidcosθ+VfidVo−Vacosθroa(roaVa)1−γ,roa=Vo2+Va2−2VoVacosθ,γ=2

Here θ is the angle between the source and the attractor, Vo and Va are their LG-frame velocities, and Vfid is the LG's infall toward the attractor. The flow-corrected velocity is Vcosmic = VLG + Σ Vin; NED adds all terms of eq. A2 with positive sign and uses negative declinations for the Great Attractor and Shapley NED. Composing frame corrections exactly

(7.7.5)(1+zhel)=(1+zCMB)(1+z⊙),z⊙≃−|𝐕⊙|cosθc
(7.7.6)1+zframe=1+zBγ(1−βcosθ),β=|𝐕⊙|c,γ=(1−β2)−1/2

Eq. (7.7.5) is the multiplicative form of Davis et al. 2011 and Carr et al. 2022. Replacing the first-order z⊙ with the special-relativistic 1 + z⊙ = γ(1 − β cos θ), θ being the catalogue (barycentric) angle between target and apex, gives eq. (7.7.6). The equivalent factor γ(1 + β cos θ) needs θ measured in the frame itself (eq. 7.8.2). The frame calculator below uses eq. (7.7.6) with each frame's |V|. For the CMB dipole γ − 1 = 7.6 × 10⁻⁷, or 0.23 km/s in cz (derived). Adding velocities instead of multiplying (1 + z) factors is the dominant error at extragalactic redshifts (worked example below; §7.8).
