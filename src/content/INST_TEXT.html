A spectrograph's wavelength scale is borrowed from a reference spectrum that is recorded alongside the science exposure or imprinted on it. For differential work (EPRV, α and μ, redshift drift) the calibrator sets the precision; the rest-wavelength catalogue only fixes the zero point. Lamp and atmospheric references inherit the 10⁻⁴–10⁻³ Å accuracy of laboratory wavelengths. Only the laser frequency comb is traceable to an atomic clock.

Hollow-cathode lamps: Th-Ar and U-Ne

  • HARPS Th-Ar list (3785–6915 Å; Lovis & Pepe 2007). Each line position is good to ≈ 10 m/s (0.18 mÅ), and the global HARPS solution is precise to 20 cm/s. Measured centroiding errors are 0.002 px (1.6 m/s) for the strongest unsaturated lines and 0.2 px (160 m/s) for the faintest, with a CCD-inhomogeneity floor of ≈ 0.01 px (8 m/s). The absolute scale is tied to the Palmer & Engleman (1983) Th atlas, whose lines are accurate to 16–82 m/s.
  • Argon lines shift from lamp to lamp by "a few tens of m/s" (pressure shifts; Whaling et al. 2002, as cited by Lovis & Pepe), i.e. 0.2–0.9 mÅ at 5500 Å, and should be avoided when high precision is needed. Th lines are "individually stable in wavelength at least within the error bars".
  • Th Ritz wavelengths (Redman, Nave & Sansonetti 2014): 19 874 lines over 250–5500 nm, from globally optimised Th I–III energy levels. The scatter about the Ritz values is 0.82 mÅ for Palmer & Engleman and 1.32 mÅ for Lovis & Pepe, so an internal precision of 0.18 mÅ is not an absolute accuracy. The best Ritz values reach ≈ 0.2 mÅ (e.g. 873.482334 ± 0.000017 nm).
  • Line selection. The older Th-Ar list left ±30–75 m/s distortions in UVES wavelength solutions. A line-selection algorithm improved the residuals by more than a factor of 3 (Murphy et al. 2007a).
  • Near-IR. The Th-Ar atlas of Kerber, Nave & Sansonetti 2008 (691–5804 nm) "is sufficient for precisions of ≈ 50 m/s on CRIRES". Uranium offers "six times as many calibration lines as thorium in the NIR": 10 100 U I/II lines over 850–4000 nm (both quotations from Redman et al. 2011). In the H band (1454–1638 nm), U-Ne residuals are 50 % larger than those of a modal-noise-limited LFC (Redman et al. 2012).

Iodine absorption cell

An I₂ cell in the stellar beam imprints a dense forest of molecular absorption lines on the spectrum. The wavelength scale and the instrumental profile are then modelled together with the star, which gave 3 m/s Doppler precision (Marcy & Butler 1992; Butler et al. 1996). The reference is the cell's own high-resolution atlas, not a set of laboratory wavelengths. The method covers only the green–yellow part of the spectrum, where I₂ absorbs strongly, and the I₂ lines contaminate the stellar lines there, so iodine-cell spectra are unsuited to absolute line positions.

Fabry–Pérot etalons

(7.9.1)mλm=2n(λ)Lcosθ

An etalon gives a dense, regular comb of peaks of order m. Their absolute positions depend on the cavity length L and its dispersion, so the etalon must be anchored to a lamp or an LFC.

  • A combined hollow-cathode + FP solution removes the 50 m/s distortions of lamp-only solutions and agrees with an LFC to ≤ 10 m/s (Wildi et al. 2010; Bauer et al. 2015).
  • ESPRESSO's Th-Ar+FP and LFC solutions still differ by up to 24 m/s, depending on wavelength (Schmidt et al. 2021).
  • Etalons drift chromatically, and the drift must be modelled (Cersullo et al. 2019; Terrien et al. 2021, HPF).

Laser frequency combs

(7.9.2)fn=f0+nfrep

The comb repetition rate frep and offset f0 are referenced to an atomic clock. Every mode frequency is then known to better than 10⁻¹² (Murphy et al. 2007b), i.e. 5 × 10⁻⁹ Å or 0.3 mm/s at 5000 Å (derived). Simulations put the photon limit near 1 cm/s over 4000 Å. Measured performance:

  • First astronomical use: ≈ 9 m/s at 1.5 µm (Steinmetz et al. 2008).
  • An "astro-comb" designed for 1 cm/s (Li et al. 2008).
  • Short-term repeatability of 2.5 cm/s on HARPS (Wilken et al. 2012).
  • Two independent LFCs confirm 1 cm/s stability (Probst et al. 2020).
  • ≲ 4 cm/s per exposure and 1 cm/s averaged over a few hours, but ≈ 60 cm/s zero-point offsets between combs in the raw data (Milaković et al. 2020).
  • ANDES assessment: ≈ 50 cm/s inconsistency between the two HARPS LFCs, and flux-dependent LFC shifts of up to ±2 m/s on ESPRESSO (Martins et al. 2024).

Atmospheric references: telluric O₂ and OH airglow

  • Telluric O₂. HARPS O₂ lines are stable to 10 m/s rms over 6 years. They reach 5 m/s "out of the box" and 1–2 m/s with simple atmospheric modelling (Figueira et al. 2010). HITRAN2024 O₂ positions have a median uncertainty bound of 0.016 mÅ (Gordon et al. 2026; §6.3). Observed lines, however, are pressure-shifted: the median is +2.1 mÅ (+81 m/s) through a sea-level atmosphere, derived from the HITRAN δair. Winds shift them too: 10 m/s is 0.25 mÅ at 7600 Å.
  • OH Meinel lines are computed from laboratory FTS energy levels (Abrams et al. 1994). They are "secure to about one hundredth of Å" (Rousselot et al. 2000), ≈ 190 m/s at 1.6 µm, and HITRAN position codes 2–3 mean 2.5–250 mÅ at 1.58 µm. At low J, independent model lists agree to 1–6 mÅ: Oliva et al. 2015 minus HITRAN has a median of +0.8 mÅ, with 68 % within 2.5 mÅ (§5.4). This is internal consistency, not independent accuracy.
  • Optical sky atlases. Osterbrock et al. 1996 and 1997 list Keck/HIRES OH lines for echelle calibration. The UVES atlas (Hanuschik 2003) quotes 17 mÅ per line, and its OH lines sit +7 to +17 mÅ from HITRAN and HIRES positions in the red settings (derived; §5.3).
  • Practical limits. Sky lines are Λ-doublets, often blended. Their thermal FWHM is ≈ 0.038 Å at 1.6 µm (190 K). They check zero points at the 0.1 km/s level, not at m/s.
