This table lists 214 transitions in 70 line groups of 20 species, from He II 303.78 Å to the He I 10830 triplet: O I, O II, N I, N II, H I, He I, He II, Na I, K I, Li I, Ca I, Ca II, Mg I, Mg II, Fe I, Fe II, Si II, Cr I, Mn I and Ar I. It covers the nightglow, twilight and dayglow lines, the main auroral and geocoronal lines, the O I recombination multiplets modelled by PALACE, and the strongest meteor lines (IMO Table 3-7; Borovička 1994). A line can belong to several categories: 121 dayglow, 85 nightglow, 51 aurora, 49 meteor, 32 twilight and 27 geocorona. Eleven lines are marked candidate (He I 5876, the Ca II IR triplet and Ar I 1048/1067): they are included for completeness, but the consulted sources report no terrestrial airglow or meteor detection of them. The PALACE model lines are listed separately in §5.3. They include the geocoronal Hγ and Hδ (0.19 and 0.11 R) and 183 O I recombination lines at 1.07–2.13 µm, which this table leaves out.

Wavelengths: NIST ASD as published

  • Every wavelength string is printed exactly as NIST ASD v5.12 gives it Kramida et al. 2024. Each window was queried twice: in standard air between 2000 and 20 000 Å, with vacuum wavenumbers, and in vacuum throughout. Both media are therefore ASD's own values, not a local conversion. Below 2000 Å ASD gives vacuum only (82 lines); that is the convention, not missing data.
  • Check: λvac/n with the Peck & Reeder 1972 index reproduces all 132 ASD air values to within one unit of the last printed digit (median 0.10 mÅ). This is the same result as for the element lines in §4.2.
  • 212 lines are Ritz (level-derived) values in both media; 2 Fe II meteor lines are observed values. Aki are ASD values, with the M1 and E2 parts summed for forbidden lines. Transition types are 186 E1, 16 E2+M1, 6 E2, 3 M1 and 3 E1+M2.
  • Velocity scale of 1 mÅ: 53.75 m s⁻¹ at 5577 Å, 246.6 m s⁻¹ at Lyα and 27.7 m s⁻¹ at 10830 Å.

For lines without a stated uncertainty, ASD's “rule of 25” puts the implied uncertainty at generally 2.5–25 units of the last digit, so 1 mÅ is not guaranteed. This applies to the 133 “implied” lines, which include the brightest nightglow lines. ASD gives no level uncertainties for O I, N I or O II either. As an external check, Keck/HIRES sky positions agree with NIST to +1 ± 3 mÅ for the nightglow atomic lines, while UVES positions are offset by +13 ± 2 mÅ Cosby et al. 2006. That offset belongs to the UVES scale, not to NIST. For Hα and Hβ, use the fine-structure components (σ ≈ 0.03 mÅ), not a multiplet value: any single value depends on how the levels are excited. The multiplet rows here are ASD's n = 2–3 and n = 2–4 entries, Ritz values between the n-labelled levels: 6562.819 ± 0.007 and 4861.333 ± 0.003 Å. The Fraunhofer table (§6.1) uses ASD's separate blend rows instead, the gA-weighted centroids 6562.795 ± 0.009 and 4861.3250 ± 0.0014 Å, which are 24 and 8 mÅ shorter. Neither is the geocoronal emission centre. In the geocorona ≈ 95 % of Hα is in the two 3p ²P → 2s ²S components at 6562.72483 and 6562.77153 Å Gardner et al. 2017; the corresponding Hβ pair (4p ²P → 2s ²S) is at 4861.286949 and 4861.297761 Å.
