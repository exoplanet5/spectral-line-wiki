The telluric list holds 23 976 lines from HITRAN2024 (Gordon et al. 2026), from 4427.33 to 24999.59 Å (vacuum). A line was kept if, as an isolated line, its central depth is ≥ 1 % after convolution with a Gaussian line-spread function of R = 10⁵, at airmass 1, for an observer at sea level.

  • Atmosphere. The model is the 62-layer AFGL-1986 U.S. Standard atmosphere (Anderson et al. 1986): PWV 14.17 mm and 344 DU of O₃. CO₂, CH₄ and N₂O are scaled to the NOAA GML 2025 global means of 425.63 ppm, 1935.95 ppb and 338.86 ppb (Lan et al. 2025).
  • Line model. Voigt profiles with HITRAN S(T) and γ(p,T), using the TIPS-2025 partition sums (Gamache et al. 2025); line mixing and continua are ignored. Weak lines pass a check: equivalent width / (S·column) = 0.997 in the median.
  • Depths. 9 557 lines reach ≥ 10 % depth and 4 371 reach ≥ 50 %. From a 2.5 km site (PWV 4.37 mm), 17 360 still reach 1 %. These counts use the unrounded depths. The table stores depths to 4 decimals, so filtering it can give a few more lines (about 30 more at 2.5 km).

Each row keeps the HITRAN vacuum wavenumber verbatim (F12.6, zero pressure), λvac = 10⁸/ν̃, and λair in standard air from Peck & Reeder 1972, the same formula as for the element lines. That formula was fitted only up to 1.69 µm and is extrapolated beyond. Ciddor (450 ppm CO₂) and the IAU/Morton formula give air wavelengths 0.04–0.31 and 0.04–0.45 mÅ shorter over 3000–25000 Å (derived). Many infrared users quote vacuum wavelengths instead.

Precision of the positions

  • HITRAN quotes every wavenumber to 10⁻⁶ cm⁻¹, but the stated uncertainty decides the precision. The commonest code, 4 (10⁻⁴–10⁻³ cm⁻¹; 13 006 lines), guarantees ≤ 1 mÅ only below 1 µm, because the bound scales as λ² (derived).
  • O₂, CO and most CO₂ lines are sub-mÅ references. H₂O is borderline: the median bound is 0.86 mÅ and 58 % of lines are ≤ 1 mÅ. CH₄ (median 5.4 mÅ) and N₂O (51 mÅ) do not meet a 1 mÅ requirement.
  • Over the whole list, 51.8 % of the lines have a bound ≤ 1 mÅ (55 % of the lines at least 10 % deep) and 12.3 % ≤ 0.1 mÅ.
  • No independent check against an observed telluric atlas was made.

Pressure shifts must be applied

HITRAN positions are for zero pressure. In the atmosphere each line moves by δair·p (HITRAN definition ν̃* = ν̃ + δairp). For an unsaturated line seen through the whole column, the relevant pressure is the S·u-weighted mean pressure of the absorbing gas, peff. That is 0.51–0.59 atm for the well-mixed gases and 0.81 atm for H₂O, which is concentrated near the ground.

ν̃₀ in cm⁻¹, δair in cm⁻¹ atm⁻¹ (mostly negative, i.e. a redshift), p in atm, λ in Å; u is the absorber column per layer.

  • Size. The median effective shift is +2.1 mÅ for O₂ and +10 to +23 mÅ for the other molecules. That is 2 to 23 times the 1 mÅ goal, so a zero-pressure list is wrong by that much unless δair is applied. The “λair + P-shift” column of the table applies it with the sea-level peff. From a high site the absorbing column has a lower mean pressure, so the shift is smaller (not tabulated).
  • δair unknown. It is zero, i.e. unknown, for 577 lines (2.4 %).
  • δair uncertain. For 71 % of the lines δair carries uncertainty code 3 (10⁻³–10⁻² cm⁻¹ atm⁻¹). That leaves ≥ 0.6 mÅ atm⁻¹ of uncertainty at 7600 Å and ≥ 2.2 mÅ atm⁻¹ at 1.5 µm (derived).
  • What the single peff neglects. The temperature dependence of δ, speed dependence, line mixing (important in the O₂ A band and the CO₂ bands), and the higher formation altitude, hence lower pressure, of saturated cores.

Winds, observer frame and solar atlases

  • Frame. Telluric lines are at rest in the air, so they sit in the topocentric frame, displaced by the line-of-sight wind. A 10 m/s wind moves a line 0.25 mÅ at 7600 Å (derived). HARPS measurements found telluric O₂ lines stable to 10 m/s rms over six years: about 5 m/s without any correction for short programmes, and 1–2 m/s once the line-of-sight wind and airmass are modelled (Figueira et al. 2010).
  • Barycentric-corrected spectra. Correcting a spectrum to the barycentre moves the telluric lines by −vB, up to ±30 km/s (±0.76 Å at 7600 Å).
  • Solar atlases. Solar atlases are Doppler-corrected to the Sun, so their telluric lines are displaced by the Earth–Sun radial velocity, and their positions differ from atlas to atlas (Reiners et al. 2016; §6.4).

Nothing below 4427 Å: continua only

The bluest selected line is H₂O at 4427.334 Å (vacuum; 4426.091 Å air). Between the ground-based UV cutoff and 4427 Å the atmosphere absorbs only as continua:

  • the O₃ Hartley–Huggins edge: vertical τ = 1 at 3088 Å and 0.01 at 3407 Å (vacuum, 223 K; Serdyuchenko et al. 2014);
  • O₂–O₂ collision-induced bands at 3439, 3610, 3803, 4465 and 4769 Å (vacuum), each < 1 % deep.

At longer wavelengths the broad features are:

  • the O₃ Chappuis band, 4.6 % at its 6024 Å peak;
  • the O₂–O₂ bands at 5777 and 6302 Å, 1.5 % and 1.0 %;
  • the O₂–air CIA at 1.27 µm, 7.0 %, beneath the discrete a–X lines.

Bands and continua

Model limits.
  • Depths and EWs are for isolated lines in a single standard atmosphere (PWV 14.2 mm at sea level). Real H₂O depths scale strongly with PWV and airmass.
  • HDO lines use HITRAN's terrestrial isotopic abundance, but atmospheric water is depleted in HDO, so their depths are overestimated.
  • Blends are not combined.
  • The 1 % threshold at R = 10⁵ is a design choice: it drops narrow stratospheric O₃ lines and very weak H₂O lines (27 778 lines reach 1 % at infinite resolution).
  • Telluric lines in the A and B bands and near 6300 Å (O₂ γ band, overlapping [O I] 6300 airglow, §5) are excellent wavelength references, but only after the pressure shift and the observer frame are accounted for.
