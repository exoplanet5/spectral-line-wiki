Once the barycentric correction (§7.4) has been applied, a velocity refers to the solar-system barycentre. The barycentre moves at ≈ 12–20 km/s relative to the local standard of rest (LSR), the value depending on the definition, and at ≈ 232–252 km/s around the Galactic centre. Each frame change in this section adds the line-of-sight projection of that solar velocity. At 5000 Å, 1 km/s is 16.7 mÅ (derived). At the mÅ level, the frame definition therefore matters far more than the measurement errors behind it.

(7.6.1). astropy issue #18550 records that the LSR docstring wording was ambiguous. The code follows Gordon: the Sun moves toward the apex.

Kinematic LSR (LSRK): the radio convention

The standard solar motion is 20 km/s toward α = 18h, δ = +30° (B1900) Gordon 1976. It is a definition, not a measurement. FITS records it as SPECSYS = 'LSRK' Greisen et al. 2006, and it is implemented as astropy LSRK and SLALIB sla_RVLSRK. Radio spectral-line velocities (H I, CO, masers) are almost always LSRK. A quoted LSRK velocity means this fixed vector, not the modern solar motion.

  • J2000/ICRS apex (derived with astropy, FK4 B1900 → ICRS including E-terms): α = 18h03m50.25s, δ = +30°00′16.8″ (270.959°, +30.005°).
  • Galactic apex: (l, b) = (56.157°, 22.765°). The equivalent (U, V, W) = (10.27, 15.32, 7.74) km/s (derived); Reid et al. 2009 quote (10.3, 15.3, 7.7). The ICRS vector (0.290, −17.317, 10.001) km/s is identical to astropy's hard-coded V_OFFSET_LSRK.
  • Using the apex as "18h, +30° J2000" displaces it by 0.83°. The correction then changes by up to 0.29 km/s, or 4.8 mÅ at 5000 Å (derived).

Dynamical LSR (LSRD)

Delhaye's peculiar solar motion is (U, V, W) = (9, 12, 7) km/s Delhaye 1965. That is 16.55 km/s toward (l, b) = (53.13°, 25.02°), or α, δ = 17h49m58.7s, +28°07′04″ (derived). It is FITS LSRD (conventional magnitude 16.6 km/s), astropy LSRD and SLALIB sla_RVLSRD. Adding Θ₀ = 220 km/s gives the RC3/NED Galactic standard of rest described below.

U⊙ spans only 8.6–11.1 km/s and W⊙ 7.0–7.7 km/s. V⊙ spans 4.8–15.3 km/s, because it is the component tied to the asymmetric-drift extrapolation. Schönrich et al. 2010 showed that the classical Strömberg-relation extrapolation is biased by the disc's radial metallicity gradient. Their value is astropy's default LSR, and Planck 2018 tabulates it as 17.9 ± 2.0 km/s toward (48° ± 7°, 23° ± 4°) Planck Collab. 2020.

How much the LSR choice matters. The vector differences between the six definitions above range from 1.4 km/s (LSRD vs Bland-Hawthorn & Gerhard) to 10.7 km/s (LSRK vs Ding et al.), which is 24–178 mÅ at 5000 Å. LSRK and Schönrich et al. differ by 3.2 km/s (54 mÅ). For a given direction the difference is the projection of these vectors. At the worked-example direction the six corrections span −0.69 to −4.95 km/s, a range of 4.26 km/s or 71 mÅ (all derived). An "LSR velocity" therefore carries a definitional ambiguity of several km/s unless the solar-motion vector is quoted. Use LSRK when comparing with radio data.

Galactic rotation and the Galactic standard of rest (GSR)

The GSR (FITS GALACTOC) is at rest relative to the Galactic centre. The Sun's velocity relative to it is the solar motion plus the circular velocity Θ₀ of the LSR toward l = 90°.

  • IAU 1985 standards: R₀ = 8.5 kpc, Θ₀ = 220 km/s Kerr & Lynden-Bell 1986. FITS uses 220 km/s toward (90°, 0°) as the conventional GALACTOC magnitude Greisen et al. 2006. NED's GSR is 232.3 km/s toward (87.8°, +1.7°), from RC3 de Vaucouleurs et al. 1991; NED. That vector is Θ₀ = 220 km/s plus LSRD (9, 12, 7), which gives 232.28 km/s toward (87.78°, 1.73°) (derived).
  • R₀ from the S-star orbits (GRAVITY): 8122 ± 31 pc (2018; astropy default) GRAVITY Collab. 2018. 8178 ± 13 (stat) ± 22 (sys) pc GRAVITY Collab. 2019. 8275 ± 9 ± 33 pc GRAVITY Collab. 2021. 8277 ± 9 (stat) pc, with systematics of ≈ 30 pc GRAVITY Collab. 2022.
  • Maser parallaxes: R₀ = 8.15 ± 0.15 kpc, Θ₀ = 236 ± 7 km/s, Θ₀ + V⊙ = 247 ± 4 km/s and (Θ₀ + V⊙)/R₀ = 30.32 ± 0.27 km/s/kpc Reid et al. 2019. The review values are R₀ = 8.2 ± 0.1 kpc, Θ₀ = 238 ± 15 km/s, Vg,⊙ = 248 ± 3 km/s and z₀ = 25 ± 5 pc Bland-Hawthorn & Gerhard 2016.
  • Sgr A* reflex motion: Sgr A* moves on the sky at −6.411 ± 0.008 mas/yr along the Galactic plane and −0.219 ± 0.007 mas/yr toward the NGP Reid & Brunthaler 2020. Assuming Sgr A* is at rest at the centre, this is the reflex of the solar orbit. Eq. (7.6.3) then gives the Sun's total azimuthal speed: 248.54 ± 0.84 km/s with the GRAVITY 2019 R₀, or 251.49 ± 1.09 km/s with the GRAVITY 2021 R₀. The vertical component is 8.49–8.59 km/s (derived).
  • astropy Galactocentric Astropy Collab. 2022. In astropy 7.2 the default, 'v4.0' ('latest'), has centre (α, δ) = (266.4051°, −28.936175°) Reid & Brunthaler 2004, R₀ = 8.122 kpc GRAVITY Collab. 2018, galcen_v_sun = (12.9, 245.6, 7.78) km/s Drimmel & Poggio 2018 and z⊙ = 20.8 pc Bennett & Bovy 2019. The older 'pre-v4.0' set uses 8.3 kpc, (11.1, 232.24, 7.25) km/s and 27 pc. galcen_v_sun is given in Galactocentric axes, centred on Sgr A* and tilted by z⊙/R₀. Projecting it as if it were Galactic (U, V, W) gives −88.211 km/s at the example instead of astropy's exact −88.175 km/s. In Galactic axes the vector is (12.88, 245.60, 7.81) km/s, and the projection error is at most 0.04 km/s (derived).

At the example direction the GSR corrections span 6.8 km/s, or 114 mÅ at 5000 Å. The full vector difference between the IAU 1985 GSR and the Sgr A* × GRAVITY 2021 vector is 19.7 km/s, ≈ 0.33 Å (both derived). The IAU 1985 Θ₀ = 220 km/s is 16–18 km/s below the current Θ₀ = 236–238 km/s, and its total solar azimuthal speed of 232 km/s compares with 247–252 km/s today. Quote the Θ₀ and solar-motion vector with every GSR velocity.
vGSR removes only the Sun's motion. The source's own Galactic orbit is still in it. R₀ does not enter eq. (7.6.1) directly; it matters only when V⊙ is inferred from a proper motion (eq. 7.6.3). The correction also varies across a field, by up to |V| × 1° ≈ 4.3 km/s per degree for the GSR (derived), so wide-field and IFU data need a per-fibre or per-spaxel correction.

Worked example: a target at α = 10h, δ = +20° is observed from Keck on 2026-03-20 10:00 UTC, with barycentric correction −16.495 km/s (§7.4). An observed topocentric RV vobs converts as vLSRK = vobs − 16.495 − 4.952 km/s and vGSR(astropy v4.0) = vobs − 16.495 − 88.175 km/s, both to first order. The Local Group, CMB and flow-model steps for the same target are in §7.7.
