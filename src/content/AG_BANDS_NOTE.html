This table has 171 rows covering the molecular systems of the night, twilight and day airglow and of the aurora:

  • N₂⁺: First Negative (10 bands) and Meinel (8)
  • N₂: First Positive (17), Second Positive (12), Vegard–Kaplan (14) and Lyman–Birge–Hopfield (23)
  • O₂: Atmospheric b–X (11), IR Atmospheric a–X (3), Herzberg I (11), Herzberg II (2) and Chamberlain (1)
  • NO: γ (10) and δ (9)
  • OH: 36 Meinel band origins, Δv = 2–9 (the Δv = 1 bands at 2.5–4.2 µm are listed line by line in §5.4)
  • Unresolved emission: the FeO orange arc, the NO₂ continuum, the HO₂ near-IR bands and the O₂ UV–blue band continuum

Wavelengths are taken as printed. The N₂ and N₂⁺ values are from Lofthus & Krupenie 1977 and the O₂ values from Krupenie 1972, both in air above 2000 Å and vacuum below. The NO γ and δ values are nominal positions from ≈ 15 Å-resolution spectra, medium unspecified Meier 1991.

Heads are not origins, and neither is a line

Within a band each branch follows a parabola in the running number m (m = J″ + 1 for R, −J″ for P). For a ¹Σ–¹Σ band:

(5.1)ν(m)=ν0+(B′+B″)m+(B′−B″)m2,νhead−ν0=−(B′+B″)24(B′−B″)
  • The band head is the turning point of one branch, where lines pile up. If B′ < B″ the head lies on the violet side of the origin and the band is red-degraded; if B′ > B″ the reverse. A head is the edge seen in low-resolution spectra. It is not a transition, and its measured position depends on resolution and rotational temperature.
  • The band origin ν₀ = T(v′) − T(v″) is a model quantity, and no line lies at it. For OH X²Π it also depends on how Tv is defined (here the spin–orbit-centred term value).
  • In this table the head–origin separations are 4.1 Å for N₂⁺ 1N (0–0) (3914.4 against 3910.34 Å) and 25.6 Å for O₂ b–X (0–0) (7593.73 against 7619.33 Å) (derived). That is 10³–10⁴ times the 1 mÅ target.

  • OH origins were derived for this page. A ²Π Hamiltonian (T, A, B, D, γ) was fitted to the HITRAN2020 ¹⁶OH X-state level energies for J ≤ 12.5, separately for each v, and ν₀ taken as T(v′) − T(v″). For v ≤ 6 the results agree with the Huber & Herzberg 1979 G(v) polynomial to 0.04 cm⁻¹. The agreement degrades for v ≥ 7, where that polynomial (valid to v = 5) no longer applies. Origins formed as T(v′) − T(v″) from the term values of Brooke et al. 2016, whose Hamiltonian defines Tv differently, are larger by B″ − B′ = 1.4–6.4 cm⁻¹, i.e. 1–7 Å (derived). The quoted precision is the fit precision within one convention, not the spread between conventions. Air values beyond 1.69 µm use Peck & Reeder outside its fitted range, and (9–7) at 2.15 µm is given in vacuum only.
  • Origin λ₀ (derived column) is 10⁸/σ₀ from the tabulated wavenumber, converted to air with Peck & Reeder where the row is in air, and rounded to the precision of σ₀. The N₂ and N₂⁺ tables pair a head wavelength with an origin wavenumber, so this column holds the second, independent feature.
  • Transcription: the N₂, N₂⁺ and O₂ entries were read from the table images of the NIST JPCRD reprints. For O₂ the tabulated λ and σ agree to ≤ 0.06 Å, which checks the transcription. The exceptions are the two parenthesised heads, (8623) and (15800). The N₂ and N₂⁺ rows could not be cross-checked this way.
  • Intensities keep their viewing condition: zenith dayglow Noxon 1968, nadir from 600 km Meier 1991, Paranal nightglow Noll et al. 2025, or auroral model per erg cm⁻² s⁻¹ Barthélemy et al. 2025.
  • Contamination: the N₂ 1P (7–4) band near 6545 Å contaminates auroral Hα photometry, and the 1P (3–2) P1 head (8542.54 Å) lies only 0.45 Å from Ca II 8542.09 (derived).
Band heads and origins identify a system and give its approximate position, but they are never 1 mÅ calibrators. For mÅ work use the individual rotational lines: OH Meinel (out to the Δv = 1 bands at 4.2 µm) and O₂ from HITRAN (§5.3–5.4) and the blue O₂ systems from the Cosby et al. models inside PALACE.
