Fraunhofer lines are the dark absorption lines of sunlight. They come from two places: the solar photosphere and chromosphere (Fe, Mg, Na, Ca, H …) and the Earth's atmosphere (O₂, H₂O and other gases, the telluric lines). At 1 mÅ neither kind sits at its laboratory wavelength. Solar lines carry the +633 m/s gravitational redshift (10.6 mÅ at 5000 Å) and a convective shift of −0.55 to 0 km/s. Telluric lines carry median pressure shifts of +2 mÅ (O₂) to +23 mÅ (CH₄) and move with the observer and the wind (§6.3–6.4).

History

  • 1802. Wollaston looked at sunlight through a narrow slit and saw a few dark lines, which he took for natural boundaries between the colours (Wollaston 1802).
  • 1814–1817. Fraunhofer, who needed fixed reference points to measure the refractive and dispersive powers of optical glass, found the lines independently. He counted about 574 between B and H and gave the strongest the letters A, a, B, C, D, E, b, F, G, H; his I marks the violet end of the spectrum, not a line (Fraunhofer 1817; English translation Ames 1898). His a is “a band” of many lines heaped together between A and B, i.e. telluric H₂O near 0.72 µm. His H covered both Ca II lines, which were later split into H and K. Optical-glass catalogues still use the letters for dispersion (nd, nF, nC, Abbe number), but the d, e, g, h, i, r, s, t and C′, F′ of glass makers are He, Hg, Cd and Cs lamp lines (SCHOTT 2003).
  • 1895–1897. Rowland's Preliminary Table of Solar Spectrum Wave-lengths covers 2975–7330 Å (Rowland 1895–97). Its wavelength scale is 0.106 Å (3000 Å) to 0.218 Å (5600 Å) too long relative to the later international standards (Smithsonian Tables 1921, Table 321). The Smithsonian letter list (Table 312) extends Fraunhofer's set with Greek α, D1–D3, E1/E2, b1–b4, d, f, G′, g, h, K, and L to U. Rowland values are given in §6.1 on his own scale, for history only.
  • 1928, 1966. The first revision of Rowland's table (Mount Wilson, 1928) converted it to the international scale. Moore, Minnaert & Houtgast (Moore et al. 1966, “MMH”, NBS Monograph 61) is the second revision: about 24 000 lines from 2935 to 8770 Å, with measured equivalent widths from the Utrecht photometric atlas of the disc centre, identifications (about 73 % of the lines, 63 elements) and photographic air wavelengths on the IAU 1928 international scale (abbreviated ISA on this page). Above 6600 Å it takes most wavelengths from Babcock & Moore 1947.
  • 1984 to today. Fourier-transform atlases replaced the photographic tables: the Kitt Peak flux atlas (Kurucz et al. 1984), the disc-centre FTS atlas of Brault & Neckel 1987 and the IAG flux atlas, which is accurate to ±10 m/s over 405–1065 nm (Reiners et al. 2016).

Solar or telluric?

Two classical tests tell the lines apart (Moore et al. 1966, §2.1). Meggers (1919) separated the solar from the telluric lines between 6500 and 9000 Å by their Doppler displacements: solar lines shift between the approaching and receding limbs of the rotating Sun, telluric lines do not. Babcock compared line strengths at high and low Sun: telluric lines strengthen with airmass and, for H₂O, with humidity. Today the identification is made against line-by-line molecular databases (Gordon et al. 2026, HITRAN2024). Telluric lines are narrow, and telluric O₂ lines are stable to 10 m/s rms over six years, or 1–2 m/s once the atmosphere is modelled (Figueira et al. 2010). That is why FTS solar atlases are wavelength-calibrated on telluric O₂ lines.

Of the lettered features, A, B and Greek α are O₂ bands; a, Z and y are H₂O; all the others are solar. D3 (He I 5876 Å) is chromospheric: an emission line at the limb and in prominences, not a disc-integrated absorption line.

The §6.1 table holds every lettered feature found in Fraunhofer's paper, in Rowland's list as reprinted in the Smithsonian tables, in the MMH notes and in modern popular lists, plus the glass makers' lamp letters. That is 39 photospheric rows, 3 chromospheric (Ca II H and K, He D3), 9 telluric (including a), the Na D doublet mean and 10 lamp lines. The band letters A and B appear as their band-head lines, and B also as Rowland's “principal line”. Each row gives:

  • the laboratory wavelength in both media, verbatim from NIST ASD v5.12 (observed or Ritz) or HITRAN, with its uncertainty: 42 rows are at ≤ 1 mÅ, and 15 have no stated uncertainty (Mg I b, Ca I, Ca II, He I and Cd I lines; their σλ is taken as 2.5 units of the last digit);
  • the separate solar values: the MMH 1966 disc-centre λ (49 rows), the APGL98 Kitt Peak flux λ (18 rows), and parabola fits to the IAG flux-atlas line cores made for this page (23 rows, 405–1065 nm, derived), with the IAG − lab shift in m/s;
  • Rowland's value on Rowland's scale, the Wikipedia value, the transition and notes on conflicts.
Letters are not unique. The sources disagree on several letters:
  • a (Fraunhofer: H₂O band) versus Greek α (Rowland: the O₂ γ-band group at 6278 Å, which Wikipedia calls “a”);
  • E1/E2 (Wikipedia's “E2” at 5270.39 Å is Rowland's E1 Fe line);
  • d (Fe I 4383.54 for Rowland, Fe I 4668.13 for Wikipedia, and also He D3) and e (Fe I 4383.54 or Hg 5460.75);
  • f (Fe I 4325.76 for Rowland, but Hγ in some lists, where Rowland has G′);
  • P (Fe for Rowland, Ti II 3361.21 in MMH and modern lists);
  • T (Fe I 3020.64 or 3021.07);
  • t (Fe I + Ni I blend);
  • Z and y, which are H₂O lines (y tentatively), not O₂ as often stated.

For a rest wavelength, use the laboratory columns of §6.1 or the element lines of §4. Solar-atlas wavelengths (MMH, APGL98, IAG, and all of §6.2) include the solar gravitational and convective shifts. They are the right reference only for calibrating spectra of the Sun or of solar twins, and only in the same observing mode (flux or disc centre). For the MMH values in §6.1, MMH − lab is +0.3 to +1.1 km/s for most unblended lines above 3800 Å (derived). Below about 3700 Å (Rowland-corrected “r” values, and Babcock's measurements below 3060 Å) MMH − lab scatters from −1.7 to +2.7 km/s, so these values are not mÅ data.
